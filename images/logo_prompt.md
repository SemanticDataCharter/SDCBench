# SDCBench Logo — Image Prompts (Nano Banana)

**What SDCBench is:** a cross-platform desktop app (Tauri) that acts as a personal "bench" to a hosted SDCStudio account. A domain expert loads data locally and assembles governed, self-describing SDC data models on a visual **Google Blockly** canvas, where family-colored blocks represent reference-model bases. Raw data stays local; only approved components sync.

**The fusion the logo needs:** **SDC** (semantic data substrate: structured, governed, architectural/blueprint feel) + **Blockly** (interlocking snap-together blocks with the signature puzzle-notch connectors, colorful, approachable, MIT/Scratch pedagogy lineage). The idea to convey: *you build rigorous, governed data by snapping meaningful blocks together.*

**Brand palette (use exactly):**
- Navy `#0A2342` (primary)
- Teal `#2CA58D` (secondary)
- Gold `#F0A500` (highlight)
- Off-white `#F7F9FB` (light background / negative space)

**Deliverable:** a clean app-icon mark that reads at small sizes, plus a horizontal wordmark lockup. Flat modern vector look with a touch of depth, not skeuomorphic, not gradient-heavy. Keep the Gemini generation watermark in place.

---

## Concept A — "Snap-Stack" (recommended)

> A modern flat-vector app logo on a rounded-square icon tile. Three interlocking blocks in the style of Google Blockly, each with the signature puzzle-notch connector (a tab on top, a matching bump on the bottom) so they clearly snap together vertically. The three blocks are colored, from top to bottom, deep navy #0A2342, teal #2CA58D, and gold #F0A500. The stack is slightly offset so its overall silhouette suggests a bold letter S. Across the face of each block run faint thin blueprint grid lines and one or two small connected nodes (tiny dots joined by short links), hinting at an underlying semantic data graph living inside the blocks. Clean geometry, subtle soft shadow for gentle depth, generous negative space, off-white #F7F9FB background. Confident, technical, friendly. Square 1:1, centered, high resolution.

## Concept B — "Block Meets Graph"

> A minimalist app-icon mark: a single rounded Blockly-style puzzle block with the recognizable top notch and bottom connector bump, in deep navy #0A2342. Emerging from inside the block is a small constellation of semantic-graph nodes, three or four teal #2CA58D dots connected by thin lines, with one gold #F0A500 node as the highlight, as if the block contains a governed data structure. The composition fuses "a code block" with "a knowledge graph." Flat vector, crisp edges, off-white #F7F9FB background, soft depth. Reads clearly at 32 pixels. Square 1:1, centered.

## Concept C — "The Bench"

> A clean, minimal logo that plays on the word "bench" (a workbench for assembling data). A simple horizontal platform line in navy #0A2342, and resting on top of it, snapped together, a short row of interlocking Blockly-style blocks with visible puzzle-notch connectors in teal #2CA58D and gold #F0A500. The blocks carry tiny field-tag marks suggesting data columns. The whole mark feels like a tidy workbench where governed data models are assembled block by block. Flat vector, lots of negative space, off-white #F7F9FB background. Square 1:1 for the icon; also works as a wide mark.

## Wordmark lockup (pair with the chosen mark)

> A horizontal logo lockup for a software product. On the left, the [chosen mark] icon. To its right, the wordmark "SDCBench" in a clean modern geometric sans-serif. Render "SDC" in deep navy #0A2342 and "Bench" in teal #2CA58D so the two halves read as one word with a subtle color break at the seam. ## Technical / style notes for whichever concept wins

- Ask for **both** an icon-only 1:1 version (for the app icon and favicon) and the 16:9 wordmark lockup.
- Also generate a **single-color navy version** and a **reversed (light mark on navy) version** for dark UI and small favicons.
- Keep it **flat vector, geometric, minimal**; avoid photoreal, avoid heavy gradients, avoid drop-shadow overload. A single soft shadow for depth is fine.
- The **puzzle-notch connector is the load-bearing Blockly cue** — make sure it is unmistakable in every concept, since that is what signals "built on Blockly."
- The **blueprint grid lines / graph nodes are the SDC cue** — subtle, not busy.
- Palette is fixed to the four hexes above; do not introduce other colors.
